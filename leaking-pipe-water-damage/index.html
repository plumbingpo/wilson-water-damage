Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Wilson

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Wilson homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Keep Power AwayWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Our Process

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Make Targeted Openings

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Structural Drying

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the framing and subfloor reach normal moisture readings, usually 3 to 5 days. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

4

Treat and Document

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Soaked Wall Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Rotted Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Once framing lumber is no longer sound, it must be removed and new wood put in.

Damaged Flooring

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Access to the pipe, with a sink cabinet being cheaper to work in than a slab or a closed wall.
  • How long the pipe leaked and how far the moisture spread from it.
  • Mold, if there is any, because containment and removal come first and drying finishes after.
  • The number of rooms and floors affected, such as a ceiling underneath the leaking line.
