Wet Carpet Drying: What Wilson Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Wilson extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.
