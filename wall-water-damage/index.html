Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Wilson

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
How We Fix It

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

The bottom of the wall is the place to begin. Common clues are baseboards that have swelled, paint that bubbles or peels, a pale water mark and a musty odor. It might feel a little soft or cool as well. Still, no sign on that list shows the full extent. That takes a moisture meter, and sometimes a thermal camera, to read behind the surface.
A flood cut is a straight, level cut through the drywall, made 12 to 24 inches above the highest wet reading. Drywall under that line comes off, which lets us pull insulation and dry the studs. A neat, even edge simplifies hanging replacement board and helps the seam disappear.
A fan dries the painted face and barely affects the cavity. Insulation, framing at the floor and the hidden face of the panel hold moisture when nothing circulates behind the surface. A dehumidifier is also needed to pull that moisture out of the room. Have it properly checked when dampness or odor lasts past a day or two.
Most walls take 3 to 5 days when professional drying equipment is used. It runs longer where moisture is held in by plaster, insulation, vinyl wall covering or multiple coats of gloss paint. Left alone with no equipment, a wall cavity can stay damp for weeks, which is long enough for mold to take hold.
