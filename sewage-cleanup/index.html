Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Wilson, Wilson Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
