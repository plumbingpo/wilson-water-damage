Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Locate the main shutoff valve now, and show each person at home how to turn it off.
  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Swap worn hoses feeding the washer, sinks and toilets rather than waiting for a rupture.
  • Any trip beyond a few days is a reason to turn the main supply off before you go.
  • Insulate pipes in unheated spaces so they do not freeze and split in cold weather.
  • Put leak alarms beneath sinks and beside the dishwasher, washer and water heater.

How to Tell You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • A growing pool on the floor is heading for the next room or running downstairs.
  • The house was flooded when you walked in, with no telling how many hours it had been going.
  • A leak upstairs is sending water down the walls of the level beneath it.
  • The tank on your water heater gave out and its contents are spreading through the room.
  • You have no way to locate or get to the shutoff while water continues to pour.
  • Sewage has backed up, or the water is dark with a foul odor.

Our Emergency Water Damage Restoration Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle emergency water damage restoration from start to finish:

1

Take Your Call

Day or night, someone picks up, finds out where the water started, which rooms are hit and what hazards exist, then gives you steps to follow until the crew arrives.

2

Shut Off the Water

First we rule out shock and contamination dangers, and then whatever feeds the water gets shut down or isolated. Until the flow ends, no other step does much good.
