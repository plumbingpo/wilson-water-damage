Flood Damage Preparation Tips in Wilson, NC

Home / Blog / Flood Prevention for Homeowners in Wilson, NC

While you can't control the weather, you can take proactive steps to protect your Wilson, NC property from flood damage. Being prepared significantly softens the blow of a flood and may save you thousands on restoration. This guide from Wilson Restoration covers the most effective preparation strategies for Wilson homeowners.

Understand the Flood Risk You Face

Preparation begins with knowing how likely your property is to flood. Look up your address on the FEMA flood maps to see if it falls inside a designated flood zone. Keep in mind that the majority of flood claims come from areas not designated as flood zones, so a lower-risk location still deserves attention. Evaluate your property's topography, drainage patterns, and proximity to waterways that could overflow during heavy rain.

Physical Steps to Protect Your Home

On the physical side, keep a sump pump with a backup battery installed and maintained, store valuables and key documents higher than ground level, grade the lot to fall away from the foundation, lift electrical panels and HVAC units above where floodwater could reach, seal any foundation cracks and add a waterproof coating, and clean gutters and downspouts while running the discharge out to 6 feet or farther from the foundation.

Insurance Preparation

A typical homeowner's insurance policy excludes damage from rising floodwater. Think about buying flood insurance from a private carrier or the NFIP well before a storm is forecast, because coverage typically starts only after a 30-day waiting period. Document your property and belongings with photos and video for insurance purposes. Store your policy paperwork where you can get to it, and learn your deductible and coverage limits.

Your Plan for a Flood Emergency

Planning has to happen before floodwater is on the way. Pack a go-bag and learn the routes you would take to evacuate. Have your insurance agent's contact information readily available. Once flooding is predicted, document how the property looks beforehand, take valuables to an upper level, and switch utilities off if you receive instructions to.
