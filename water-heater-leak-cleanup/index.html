Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Shared Garage Wall

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.
